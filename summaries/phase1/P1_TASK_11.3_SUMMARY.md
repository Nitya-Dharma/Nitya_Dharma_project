Task 11.3 establishes reporting rules for Pandits in Module 11 — Governance, Moderation & Appeals. ## Core Principles Established

### Reporting Philosophy
**Key Principle:** Reporting is a signal, not a verdict.

Reports trigger review — they do not trigger punishment.

---

## Who Can Report (LOCKED)

Only the following may report a Pandit:
- ✅ Registered Users
- ✅ Other Pandits (logged-in)
- ❌ Guests may NOT report
- ❌ Anonymous reports are NOT allowed

---

## What Can Be Reported (LOCKED)

Pandits may be reported only for:
- Misleading profile information
- Inappropriate conduct
- Policy violations
- Repeated abuse patterns
- Platform safety concerns

**Key Rule:** Reports must relate to behavior or policy, not competition.

---

## What Must Never Be Reportable (LOCKED)

Reports must NEVER be allowed for:
- ❌ Low ratings
- ❌ Business competition
- ❌ Personal dislike
- ❌ Search position
- ❌ Availability status
- ❌ Trust display labels

**Key Rule:** Reporting cannot be weaponized.

---

## Reporting Scope Boundaries (LOCKED)

Reporting must NEVER:
- Auto-block a Pandit
- Auto-hide a profile
- Delete Pandit content
- Affect ranking or search
- Influence recommendations
- Trigger penalties without review

---

## Reporting Principles (LOCKED)

1. **Signal-Only Principle:** Reports create a review queue. No immediate action.

2. **Abuse-Resistance Principle:** Reports are logged. Patterns are reviewed by humans. No report-count thresholds.

3. **Fairness Principle:** Every report is reviewable. Every action is appealable. No silent enforcement.

---

## Module Boundary Alignment

- **Module 11 (Governance):** Owns report review
- **Module 9 (Reviews):** Separate reporting rules
- **Module 10 (Trust Display):** Reflects state only
- **Module 7 (Search):** ❌ No impact
- **Module 15 (Recommendations):** ❌ No impact

**Key Insight:** Reports never bypass governance review.
