# Module 17 — Notifications & Communication System — All Tasks Summary

**Module:** 17 — Notifications & Communication System  
**Status:** All 17 Tasks Defined (Ready to Lock)

---

## Overview

Module 17 defines Notifications as a delivery mechanism only, a reactive signal never a decision-maker, and a user-awareness tool not a control layer. Notifications are event-driven delivery messages, passive information carriers, contextual alerts generated by other modules, and read-only reflections of system events.

---

## Core Principles

### Notifications Philosophy
- **Notification = awareness, not authority**
- **Notifications deliver information — they do not process, decide, or enforce**
- **Events create notifications. Notifications do not create events.**
- **Notifications inform users, not algorithms.**
- **Notifications are ephemeral signals — not records of truth.**
- **Notifications are best-effort, never authoritative.**
- **Notifications remain delivery-only — permanently.**

### Hard Boundaries
- ❌ NO chat system
- ❌ NO messaging threads
- ❌ NO marketing automation
- ❌ NO behavioral nudging engine
- ❌ NO workflow orchestration
- ❌ NO business logic execution
- ❌ NO ranking, trust, or recommendation influence

## All 17 Tasks

### ✅ Task 17.1 — Notification Strategy & Scope Boundaries
**Purpose:** Formally define notifications as a delivery mechanism only, a reactive signal never a decision-maker, and a user-awareness tool not a control layer.

**Key Definitions:**
- **Notifications ARE:** Event-driven delivery messages, Passive information carriers, Contextual alerts generated by other modules, Read-only reflections of system events
- **Notifications are NOT:** Conversations, Commands, Guarantees, Requests for action enforcement, Approval or rejection signals, A replacement for dashboards

**Design Principles:**
1. Delivery-Only Principle
2. Event-Owned Principle
3. Non-Authoritative Principle
4. User-Respect Principle

---

### ✅ Task 17.2 — Notification Types Classification
**Purpose:** Create a finite, well-defined taxonomy of notification types, prevent uncontrolled notification sprawl, ensure every notification has clear semantic meaning, and avoid future misuse of notifications as workflows or controls.

**Supported Notification Types:**
1. **System Notifications** — Inform users about platform-level system states
2. **Governance Notifications** — Reflect decisions or states from Module 11 — Governance
3. **Page Update Notifications** — Triggered by Module 16 — Pages
4. **Booking Intent Notifications** — Triggered by Module 8 — Booking Intent
5. **Review & Interaction Notifications** — Triggered by Module 9 — Reviews

**Forbidden Notification Types:**
- Marketing notifications, Promotional campaigns, Engagement nudges, Behavioral prompts, "Urgent" / "High priority" classes, Recommendation-based alerts

---

### ✅ Task 17.3 — User Roles & Notification Eligibility Rules
**Purpose:** Prevent notification leakage across roles, ensure users receive only relevant information, protect privacy and governance boundaries, and keep notifications predictable and respectful.

**Supported Roles:**
1. **End Users** — May receive: Booking intent notifications (their own), Review-related notifications (their actions), Followed Page updates, System notifications
2. **Pandits** — May receive: Booking intent notifications, Reviews about their profile, Governance decisions affecting them, System notifications
3. **Page Followers** — May receive: Page update notifications, Event announcements, Page status changes
4. **Admin / Super Admin** — May receive: Governance workflow notifications, System alerts (informational), Audit visibility events

**Eligibility Principles:**
1. Least Awareness Principle
2. Role Isolation Principle
3. Context Ownership Principle
4. Non-Escalation Principle

---

### ✅ Task 17.4 — Trigger Sources & Event Ownership
**Purpose:** Prevent notifications from becoming a control layer, enforce single-source event ownership, avoid hidden logic or inferred triggers, and guarantee clean one-way dependencies.

**Authorized Trigger Sources:**
1. Module 8 — Booking Intent
2. Module 9 — Reviews
3. Module 11 — Governance
4. Module 16 — Pages
5. Module 5 — Dashboards (VISIBILITY ONLY)

**Forbidden Trigger Sources:**
- Module 14 — Analytics, User engagement metrics, Search behavior, Recommendation outputs, Time-based jobs, Notification delivery state

**Event Ownership Principles:**
1. Single-Owner Principle
2. Completion-Only Principle
3. Non-Derivation Principle
4. One-Way Dependency Principle

---

### ✅ Task 17.5 — Page Follow & Event Notification Rules
**Purpose:** Define clean, predictable Page notification behavior, protect users from spam, prevent Pages from becoming promotion engines, and keep Page notifications strictly event-based.

**Allowed Page Notification Triggers:**
- New Page Post Published
- New Informational Event Posted
- Critical Page Status Change

**Forbidden Page Notification Triggers:**
- Page views, Follower count changes, Profile edits, Content edits, Analytics milestones, Engagement thresholds, Search appearances

**Anti-Spam & Safety Principles:**
1. Opt-In Principle
2. Event-Only Principle
3. No-Replay Principle
4. No-Digest Principle
5. Governance Override Principle

---

### ✅ Task 17.6 — Booking Intent Notification Rules
**Purpose:** Keep booking intent communication informational only, prevent notification-driven workflows, preserve user and Pandit autonomy, and maintain strict separation from booking logic (Module 8).

**Allowed Booking Intent Notifications:**
1. User → Pandit — Triggered when a booking intent is submitted by a user
2. Pandit → User — Triggered when a Pandit responds to a booking intent

**Forbidden Booking Notification Behavior:**
- Confirm a booking, State availability as guaranteed, Imply service commitment, Trigger reminders or countdowns, Escalate non-responses, Penalize either party, Drive booking workflows

**Safety & Neutrality Principles:**
1. Informational-Only Principle
2. Non-Binding Principle
3. No-Escalation Principle
4. Governance Supremacy Principle

---

### ✅ Task 17.7 — Review & Interaction Notification Rules
**Purpose:** Inform relevant parties of review activity, prevent review-driven conflicts, avoid trust, ranking, or governance leakage, and keep review handling neutral and safe.

**Allowed Review Notifications:**
1. Pandit Notification — Triggered when a new review is submitted for their profile
2. Admin / Governance Notification — Triggered when a review is reported

**Forbidden Review Notification Behavior:**
- Reveal reviewer identity beyond policy, Encourage retaliation, Highlight sentiment or rating changes, Trigger visibility changes, Affect trust display, Affect recommendations or search

**Safety & Fairness Principles:**
1. Neutrality Principle
2. Anti-Retaliation Principle
3. Governance Isolation Principle
4. Trust Isolation Principle

---

### ✅ Task 17.8 — Governance & Moderation Notification Rules
**Purpose:** Inform affected parties of governance outcomes, preserve neutrality and fairness, prevent notification-driven escalation or retaliation, and maintain strict separation between decision-making and communication.

**Allowed Governance Notifications:**
1. Report-Related Notifications — Triggered when a report is submitted or status changes
2. Restriction & Block Notifications — Triggered when a Page, Pandit, or content is limited or blocked
3. Appeal Status Notifications — Triggered when an appeal is received or decision is finalized

**Forbidden Governance Notification Behavior:**
- Reveal internal moderator reasoning, Rank severity or guilt, Trigger secondary actions, Influence trust or search, Invite retaliation or public response, Replace governance dashboards

**Fairness & Safety Principles:**
1. Neutral Language Principle
2. Minimum Disclosure Principle
3. Authority Preservation Principle
4. Non-Retaliation Principle

---

### ✅ Task 17.9 — Notification Delivery Channels
**Purpose:** Define supported notification delivery channels, ensure delivery mechanisms remain interchangeable, prevent channel-based logic creep, and preserve user respect and safety.

**Supported Delivery Channels:**
1. In-App Notifications (PRIMARY) — Default delivery channel, Visible inside platform UI, Read/unread state supported
2. Email Notifications (FUTURE-READY) — Optional, Subject to user preferences, Non-promotional
3. Push Notifications (FUTURE-READY) — Optional, Mobile-first, Respect OS-level permissions

**Forbidden Delivery Behavior:**
- Change notification content, Add urgency or priority labels, Reorder notifications by engagement, Act as fallback logic, Be used for promotions or campaigns

**Delivery Principles:**
1. Channel-Neutral Principle
2. Replaceability Principle
3. User-Control Principle
4. Best-Effort Principle

---

### ✅ Task 17.10 — Notification Visibility, Read State & Expiry Rules
**Purpose:** Define how notifications appear to users, establish clear read / unread semantics, prevent notification overload, and ensure notifications remain temporary awareness signals.

**Read / Unread State Rules:**
- Notifications begin as unread
- A notification becomes read only by explicit user action
- Read state is user-specific
- Unread does NOT imply urgency, require action, or escalate visibility elsewhere

**Visibility & Display Rules:**
- Notifications are shown in chronological order
- Notifications are not grouped by severity
- Notifications are not color-coded by importance
- Notifications are not promoted or pinned automatically

**Expiry & Lifecycle Rules:**
- Notifications are time-bound
- Old notifications may expire automatically
- Expiry does NOT affect system state
- Notifications must NEVER be stored indefinitely, Replace dashboards or audit logs, Act as proof of delivery, Be re-surfaced after expiry

---

### ✅ Task 17.11 — User Notification Preferences & Opt-out Rules
**Purpose:** Respect user autonomy, prevent notification fatigue, maintain transparency and consent, and separate mandatory system alerts from optional notifications.

**Notification Category Control:**
- Users MAY control: Page update notifications, Review & interaction notifications, Booking intent notifications (non-critical), Channel preferences (in-app / email / push)
- Users MUST NOT disable: Critical governance notifications, Security-related system alerts, Account safety notifications

**Opt-out Rules:**
- Opt-out must be explicit, reversible, and respected immediately
- Notifications must NEVER: Re-enable silently, Require justification, Penalize users, Affect visibility, ranking, or trust

**User-Respect Principles:**
1. Transparency Principle
2. Granularity Principle
3. Safety Override Principle

---

### ✅ Task 17.12 — Notification × Security Boundaries
**Purpose:** Ensure notifications are safe to deliver, safe to render, cannot be abused as a data-leak surface, and cannot be used as a security side-channel.

**Payload Security Rules:**
Notifications must NEVER include:
- Personally identifiable information (PII)
- Phone numbers, emails, addresses
- Internal database IDs
- Trust scores or governance details
- Booking or payment data
- Security-sensitive flags

**Rendering & Display Safety:**
- No raw HTML execution
- No script injection
- No unsafe links or embeds
- No inline execution contexts

**Authorization & Access Rules:**
- Notifications must NEVER: Grant access to restricted resources, Replace authentication checks, Reveal existence of protected objects, Bypass role-based access control

---

### ✅ Task 17.13 — Notification × Analytics Boundaries
**Purpose:** Ensure notifications remain event-driven not metric-driven, analytics remains downstream-only, and no engagement-based amplification occurs.

**Analytics Observation Rules:**
- Analytics MAY observe: Notification sent, Notification delivered, Notification opened (aggregate only)
- Analytics must NEVER observe: Notification content meaning, User response patterns, Engagement quality, Per-user behavior, Cross-session interaction

**No Feedback Loop Guarantee:**
Analytics data must NEVER be used to:
- Reorder notifications
- Change notification frequency
- Select recipients
- Escalate delivery channels
- Optimize for clicks or engagement

---

### ✅ Task 17.14 — Notification × Governance & Trust Boundaries
**Purpose:** Ensure notifications reflect decisions already made, never shape perception of trust, never amplify or suppress authority, and never act as a parallel governance channel.

**Hard Boundaries — Governance:**
Notifications must NEVER:
- Announce decisions before governance finalization
- Include reasoning, deliberation, or reviewer identity
- Invite negotiation or response via notification
- Override appeal processes
- Suggest reversibility or guarantees

**Hard Boundaries — Trust:**
Notifications must NEVER:
- Imply verification or endorsement
- Reference trust scores or badges
- Suggest safety or quality
- Use phrases like "approved", "trusted", "verified"

---

### ✅ Task 17.15 — Failure Handling & Delivery Guarantees
**Purpose:** Ensure core system behavior does not depend on notifications, failures do not cause business or governance errors, and users are never misled by delivery assumptions.

**Notification Delivery IS:**
- Best-effort, Asynchronous, Non-blocking, Retry-capable (bounded)

**Notification Delivery is NOT:**
- Guaranteed, Transactional, A source of truth, A confirmation mechanism

**Failure Handling Principles:**
1. Non-Blocking Principle
2. No Guarantee Principle
3. Retry-with-Bounds Principle
4. Silent Degradation Principle

**Forbidden Failure Patterns:**
- Retry indefinitely, Escalate channels automatically, Trigger alerts to users on failure, Act as audit evidence, Be required for compliance

---

### ✅ Task 17.16 — Notification Audit & Traceability Rules
**Purpose:** Ensure notification activity can be internally audited, delivery events are traceable for debugging and compliance, audits do not leak into user experience, and audit data does not become a control surface.

**Notification Audit IS:**
- Internal traceability of notification events
- Metadata-level observability
- Admin-only visibility
- Post-event inspection capability

**Notification Audit is NOT:**
- A user-facing history
- Proof of delivery
- A compliance substitute
- A business decision input

**Hard Audit Boundaries:**
Notification audit logs must NEVER:
- Be exposed to end users
- Contain sensitive PII
- Contain message content beyond minimal metadata
- Be used to confirm user actions
- Be consumed by business logic
- Influence governance, trust, or ranking

**Audit Access Rules:**
- Who MAY access: Super Admins, Authorized platform auditors
- Who MUST NEVER access: End users, Pandits, Page owners, Page followers

---

### ✅ Task 17.17 — Notification × Future Expansion Compatibility
**Purpose:** Ensure notifications can evolve without refactoring, new channels can be added without logic changes, expansion does not introduce authority, and all future capabilities remain delivery-only.

**Notification Future Compatibility IS:**
- Structural readiness
- Channel extensibility
- Policy-preserving expansion
- Backward-compatible evolution

**Notification Future Compatibility is NOT:**
- Feature enablement
- Automation pathways
- Intelligence upgrades
- Authority escalation

**Hard Future Boundaries:**
Notifications must NEVER evolve into:
- Chat or messaging systems
- Marketing or engagement engines
- Workflow orchestration layers
- Trust or recommendation signals
- Analytics-driven nudging systems

**Allowed Future Extensions (POLICY-ONLY):**
- Additional delivery channels (SMS, push, email)
- Multi-language payloads
- Platform-specific renderers (web, mobile)
- Subscription-based categories (opt-in)

**Future-Safety Principles:**
1. Backward Compatibility Principle
2. Opt-In Expansion Principle
3. Authority Containment Principle
4. Isolation Preservation Principle
